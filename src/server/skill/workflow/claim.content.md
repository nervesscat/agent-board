1. `get_project` (`PROJECT`): its `instructions` win over your defaults, and its `agents` are the team: a step's instructions are written on the Team page, so brief the agent of each step with them. A Content ticket is a draft the human reads and answers, so it has no spec agent and nothing to install.
2. `get_ticket`: the instructions, the attachments (read each image and file at its local `path`), the posts, the answered decisions, and the **summary first** when there is one. Read every post and each reply under it, the human's above all: they are part of the ticket, not background. This is the ticket's context, and every brief you write carries it: its instructions, the human's posts and replies, the decisions with their answers, and the attachments. Its `complexity` is this run's level, and a Content ticket with no review has no rounds of review to size for it.
3. Claim the ticket with `claim_ticket` (`as: AS` when given). A draft that is a file in a repo the ticket names is written in a worktree of its own branch, as the build step says; otherwise the draft is text, kept on the ticket. You never change a ticket's complexity or its workflow preset.
4. `add_workspace` for any worktree you open, with its `path` and `branch` at once. On `RETRY`, start from the summary and the posts, and fix what `RETRY` names before anything else.
